# StayXI Property Pilot Program

**Purpose:** Validate that StayXI can reliably reduce routine, after-hours guest questions for independent vacation-rental operators—and that operators will pay to keep using it.

**Pilot format:** 30 days, 3–5 properties, one property per participating operator to start.

> This pilot covers the current automated property-specific text-support service only. Confirm the working contact information, SMS setup, and written pilot terms with each operator before accepting a property.

## Pilot offer

StayXI will configure a property-specific guest text line using information approved by the operator. Guests can text routine questions and receive answers from that property’s information. Questions the system cannot answer confidently are escalated to the operator.

- **Pilot fee:** $49 per property for the first 30 days, setup included.
- **Payment:** Invoice manually before the pilot begins. There is no automatic renewal.
- **After 30 days:** The operator may stop or discuss a separately priced continuation. No ongoing monthly price has been set.
- **Setup:** StayXI prepares the approved property answer library and tests the line with the operator.
- **Guest costs:** SMS and data rates may apply under the guest’s mobile plan.

The $49 amount is a price to test during this pilot, not a validated long-term price.

## What is included

- A guest-facing SMS number and property-specific answers.
- Routine question handling from the information the operator approves.
- Escalation of unsupported or uncertain questions to the operator’s configured contact.
- An admin operations dashboard with message volume, open escalations, AI failures, and measured host reply time where recorded by the system.
- A simulation-only message test console and JSON import/export for a property's answer library.
- A kickoff conversation and an end-of-pilot review.

## What is not included

Unless a separate arrangement is made in writing, the pilot does **not** include:

- A human StayXI agent answering guests 24/7.
- Emergency response, dispatch, or guaranteed host response times.
- Live weather or hurricane-alert service.
- Restaurant reservations, attraction tickets, grocery delivery, or pre-stocking.
- PMS, booking-channel, smart-lock, or payment integrations.
- A guarantee that every guest question can be answered or that every message will be delivered.

The operator remains responsible for the property, guest safety, emergency instructions, the accuracy of supplied information, and responding to escalations.

## What happens after launch

1. **Before guests use the line:** StayXI and the operator configure one property's approved answers and escalation contact, test supported and unsupported questions plus HELP/STOP, and get the operator's approval.
2. **During the 30 days:** The operator shares the guest-initiated SMS number. The automated service answers from approved property information and escalates uncertain questions; it is not a staffed guest-support line. StayXI and the operator review activity, answer quality, escalations, and feedback weekly.
3. **At closeout:** Review activity and delivery costs, discuss recurring guest needs, then stop or separately agree on a continuation and its price. There is no automatic renewal and no established ongoing price.

## Guest-needs discovery and future add-ons

The pilot also tests what additional help guests and operators actually need. StayXI will manually review available message and escalation records with operator feedback; there is no automated need classifier in the current product. Record recurring themes, approximate frequency, whether the current service answered or escalated them, operator impact, and a possible solution.

Keep the internal needs backlog aggregated: do not copy guest names, phone numbers, access codes, or full transcripts into it. Guest messages are still handled under the [Privacy Policy](./site/privacy.html); operators should explain the pilot's product-improvement use before launch.

Potential add-ons to evaluate—not included in the pilot, available for purchase, or promised—include:

- **Guest-question insights:** A grouped summary of common questions, unanswered topics, and escalation patterns, with suggested property-guide improvements. The existing message records could support an initial report after privacy and implementation review.
- **Answer-library tune-ups:** Separately scoped help preparing and maintaining operator-approved property information.
- **Portfolio insights:** A combined view of guest-question and escalation trends across an operator's properties, extending the existing per-property dashboard.

Prioritize an add-on only when recurring needs and operator willingness to pay are evidenced, privacy and safety are addressed, and delivery effort and cost are understood. Build and test it before offering it with a separately disclosed price; pilot participation does not guarantee that a request will become a feature.

### Longer-term product sequence to validate

These are engineering and discovery candidates—not planned releases or offers:

1. **Stabilize the support core:** Validate safety routing, host escalation/reply delivery, privacy retention, and operational metrics with pilot operators.
2. **Maintenance intake:** Collect a concise issue description and urgency, then route it to the operator. Before offering it, define emergency exclusions, photo handling, duplicate prevention, and delivery/acknowledgment behavior.
3. **Check-in/check-out reminders:** Only consider this after reliable reservation data and guest consent are available. Without a booking integration or an operator-maintained schedule, reminders must not be promised.
4. **Local-service links:** Start with operator-approved, clearly labeled links rather than reservations or fulfillment. Any referral fees, availability, and responsibility must be disclosed.
5. **Damage reports:** Support host-directed fact collection only; do not infer fault, assess charges, or make decisions from guest messages or photos.
6. **Key pickup and property readiness:** Explore approved static pickup instructions first; readiness checks require an operator-owned checklist and an explicit way to record completion.

Each workflow must have a named operator owner, a safe fallback, privacy limits, and measured willingness to pay before development is prioritized.

## Who to recruit

Start with independent hosts and small vacation-rental managers in Davenport, Kissimmee, and the greater Orlando area. Prioritize operators who:

- Receive routine guest questions by text, especially after hours.
- Can approve property information and make a decision about trying a service.
- Can designate a reachable escalation contact during the pilot.
- Are willing to review sample answers and provide brief weekly feedback.

Begin with a 15-minute problem interview before pitching the pilot. Ask about recent examples and their current process; do not assume AI is their main concern.

### Outreach message

> Hi [Name], I’m testing StayXI, a guest text line for vacation rentals. It answers routine questions using property information the host approves and escalates questions it can’t answer confidently. I’m inviting a few Central Florida operators to try it on one property for 30 days. Could I ask how you currently handle guest questions after hours in a 15-minute conversation?

Use a verified public business email or business phone. Personalize the message, follow the recipient’s contact preferences, and stop outreach if they decline. Do not send unsolicited marketing texts. The `hello@stayxi.com` mailbox must be confirmed working before using it as a reply address.

### Discovery questions

1. What guest questions did you receive most recently after hours?
2. How many guest messages do you handle in a typical week or stay?
3. Which questions take the most time, and which are risky to answer incorrectly?
4. What happens when you are unavailable?
5. What tools or message automation do you already use?
6. What would have to improve for you to pay to keep a service like this?

## Operator commitments

Before launch, the operator agrees to:

- Provide accurate, current property answers and identify information that must not be shared automatically.
- Review and approve the answer library, sample replies, opt-out/help wording, and escalation destination.
- Provide a reachable escalation contact and tell StayXI about changes that could affect guest answers.
- Tell StayXI whether the guest-facing text line fits the operator’s guest communications.
- Understand that StayXI may review message and escalation trends, with host feedback, to prioritize future features as described in the Privacy Policy.
- Give brief weekly feedback and participate in an end-of-pilot review.
- Not submit payment-card details, health information, or other unnecessary sensitive guest data through the text line.

StayXI and the operator should document the property, dates, pilot fee, escalation expectations, data/contact responsibilities, and cancellation terms in a written pilot agreement before the service is used with guests. This document is an operating plan, not a substitute for that agreement or legal advice.

## StayXI launch checklist

Do not put a property into guest use until each item is checked:

- [ ] Confirm the operator, property, pilot dates, fee, payment, and written terms.
- [ ] Confirm a working business contact address and phone for the operator and StayXI.
- [ ] Confirm the deployed SMS endpoint, Twilio number, signature validation, and outbound replies are working.
- [ ] Confirm guest-facing HELP and STOP handling and the applicable SMS terms/privacy notice.
- [ ] Explain that the pilot reviews message and escalation trends to identify future product needs; do not promise specific add-ons or release dates.
- [ ] Create or verify the property record and mark it live only after tests pass.
- [ ] Collect only the facts needed to answer routine guest questions.
- [ ] Keep secrets such as entry codes in the intended protected configuration; test exact matches and ensure they are not exposed to AI.
- [ ] Test common questions, unsupported questions, high-risk questions, escalation delivery, and opt-out with the operator.
- [ ] Test the host reply command from the configured phone and confirm the response reaches only its assigned guest escalation.
- [ ] Obtain operator approval of all answers and guest-facing instructions.
- [ ] Give the operator a way to report incorrect answers and pause the line promptly.
- [ ] Confirm the guest-facing demo does not expose a placeholder phone number or sample property codes as real contact/property information.

If an access-code, safety, privacy, or material-answering error occurs, pause that property’s automated service, notify the operator, investigate, and agree on a safe restart before reactivation. Never treat an uncertain answer as a successful answer.

## Measurement plan

Record a simple baseline with each operator before launch. During the pilot, review the property’s analytics and the operator’s feedback weekly. Do not count website demo activity or sales leads as property usage.

| Measure | How to record it |
|---|---|
| Guest message volume | Incoming guest messages for the property |
| Automated answers | Incoming questions answered from approved property information or AI |
| Escalations | Questions routed to the operator for a human answer |
| Answer quality | Operator review of a sample of answers: correct, incomplete, or incorrect |
| Host effort | Operator estimate of time spent on guest questions before and during the pilot |
| Safety/privacy issues | Any incorrect access, safety, privacy, or policy answer; record and investigate every incident |
| Adoption | Whether the operator shared the line with guests and guest usage occurred |
| Willingness to pay | Whether the operator would continue after the pilot at a separately agreed price |
| Product needs | Recurring question themes, approximate frequency, current handling, and operator-stated value |
| Cost to serve | SMS, AI, onboarding, support, and other direct per-property costs |

### Proposed pilot decision gates

These are hypotheses to test, not historical results or guarantees:

- **Safety gate:** Zero incorrect access-code disclosures and zero unaddressed critical safety/privacy incidents.
- **Quality gate:** At least 90% of reviewed supported answers are rated correct by the operator; unsupported questions should be escalated rather than guessed.
- **Value gate:** The operator reports a meaningful reduction in time spent on routine guest questions, targeting at least 50% versus the baseline where the volume permits a fair comparison.
- **Commercial gate:** At least 2 of the first 3 participating operators (or 3 of 5) explicitly choose to continue under a separately disclosed offer after day 30.
- **Margin gate:** Direct delivery and support costs are known and leave a viable gross margin at the price operators are willing to pay.

If message volume is too low to assess a measure, mark it **inconclusive** rather than treating it as a pass. A safety-gate failure means pause and fix before expanding.

## 30-day cadence

| Timing | Activity |

| Before day 1 | Interview, agree on terms, collect baseline, configure and test, obtain approval, collect pilot fee |
| Days 1–7 | Start with one property; check message delivery and escalation flow with the operator |

| Days 28–30 | Review results and costs; ask whether the operator would consider a separately priced continuation |
| After day 30 | Stop or continue only with the operator’s explicit agreement at a separately disclosed price |

## End-of-pilot questions

- Did StayXI reduce the time or interruptions caused by routine guest questions?
- Which answers were useful, missing, or incorrect?
- Were escalations timely and actionable?
- Did you feel comfortable sharing the text line with guests?
- What did StayXI cost you in time or other work?
- Would you continue after the pilot at a separately agreed price? What monthly price would feel fair?
- What one change would make this materially more valuable?

## Go / no-go review

After the first 3–5 properties, expand only if the safety gate passes, operators choose to continue for specific value, and delivery costs are understood. If not, use the interviews and scorecards to narrow the customer segment, fix reliability or onboarding, or change the offer before investing in more features or outreach.
